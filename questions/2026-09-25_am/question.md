# The Polynomial That Takes Integer Values at Half-Integers

Let $p(x)$ be a polynomial with real coefficients such that $p\!\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)$ is an integer for every integer $k$. Must $p(x)$ have rational coefficients?

Moreover, suppose $p(x)$ has degree $n$ and $p\!\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)$ is an integer for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Show that $2^n \cdot p(x)$ has integer coefficients (i.e., the denominator of each coefficient of $p$ divides $2^n$).
