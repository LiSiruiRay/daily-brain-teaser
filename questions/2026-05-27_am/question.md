# The Ballot Problem's Cousin: Leading Throughout

You and your opponent are in an election. You receive $a$ votes and your opponent receives $b$ votes, with $a > b$. The votes are counted one by one in a uniformly random order.

**What is the probability that you are strictly ahead of your opponent throughout the entire counting process (i.e., your running total strictly exceeds theirs after every single vote is counted)?**

*(This is a classic — derive it from first principles, not by citing a theorem.)*
