# The Plane Cannot Be Sliced into Circles

A **circle** here means an ordinary round circle $\{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 : |x - c| = r\}$ with radius $r > 0$. It is a curve, not a filled disk.

**Question.** Can the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ be written as a union of pairwise **disjoint** circles?

Prove your answer.

*(Bonus to think about afterwards: what happens in $\mathbb{R}^3$?)*

---

**Field:** Topology (compactness / nested sets in the plane)

**Why it's interesting:** The question sounds like it should need heavy machinery, such as the Euler characteristic or a fixed-point theorem. In fact it falls to a five-line argument using only nested compact sets. The contrast with $\mathbb{R}^3$ makes it more striking: three-space *can* be partitioned into disjoint circles.
