# The Prisoner's Dilemma

Three prisoners $A$, $B$, and $C$ have applied for parole. The board will release exactly **two** of the three, and each prisoner knows this, but not which two.

Prisoner $A$ asks a warder (who knows the decision) to name **one prisoner other than $A$ himself** who will be released. The warder says: **"$B$ will be released."**

Prisoner $A$ reasons: *"Before asking, my chance of release was $\tfrac{2}{3}$. Now I know it's either $\{A,B\}$ or $\{B,C\}$, each equally likely — so my chance has dropped to $\tfrac{1}{2}$. I should not have asked!"*

**Is $A$'s reasoning correct? What is $A$'s actual probability of release after the warder's answer?**

*(Assume: if both $B$ and $C$ are to be released, the warder picks uniformly at random between naming $B$ or naming $C$.)*
