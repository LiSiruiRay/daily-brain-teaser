# The Uniform Limit of Differentiable Functions Need Not Be Differentiable — But Its Integral Remembers

Let $f_n : [0,1] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a sequence of continuously differentiable functions converging **uniformly** to $f$.

**(a)** Must $f$ be differentiable? Give a brief justification or counterexample.

**(b)** Now suppose additionally that $f_n'$ converges **uniformly** to some function $g$. Prove that $f$ is differentiable and $f' = g$.

The proof of (b) hides a beautiful trick: instead of differentiating, **integrate**.
