# The Nowhere-Zero Derivative Paradox

Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be differentiable everywhere, and suppose $f'(x) \neq 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

Must $f'$ have constant sign? That is, must either $f'(x) > 0$ for all $x$, or $f'(x) < 0$ for all $x$?

Prove or find a counterexample.
