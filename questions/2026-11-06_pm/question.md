# Nonnegative Polynomials Are Sums of Squares

Let $p(x)$ be a polynomial with real coefficients such that

$$p(x) \ge 0 \quad \text{for every real } x.$$

Prove that there exist real polynomials $f_1(x), \dots, f_k(x)$ with

$$p(x) = \sum_{j=1}^{k} f_j(x)^2 .$$

**Bonus:** Show that $k = 2$ always suffices.

**Food for thought:** Does the same statement hold for polynomials in two variables?

---

**Field:** Algebra / real polynomials (Putnam)

**Why it's interesting:** The converse is obvious, since a sum of squares is nonnegative. The question is whether nonnegativity is always *explained* by squares. In one variable it is, and the proof is a short use of complex numbers. In several variables it fails, which leads to Hilbert's 17th problem.
