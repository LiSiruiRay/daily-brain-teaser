this project is for helping me find small but beautiful mathematical problems.

My goal is not exam preparation or structured training. I simply want to keep my mathematical thinking alive and enjoy elegant ideas.

Please select a few problems that satisfy the following requirements:

Difficulty / Time:
- A typical mathematician with strong undergraduate math background should be able to attempt the problem within 5 minutes.
- The key idea or solution should be understandable within about 15 minutes.

Quality requirements:
- The problem should be interesting, elegant, tricky, surprising, or conceptually deep.
- Prefer problems that illustrate a beautiful idea or technique.
- Avoid routine computational exercises or long technical proofs.

Sources:
- Problems do NOT need to come only from textbook exercises.
- They may come from:
  - classic textbooks
  - famous problems
  - mathematical folklore
  - Putnam / Olympiad warmups
  - integration bee problems
  - probability puzzles
  - conceptual ML/statistics questions
  - famous counterexamples
  - elegant proofs or mathematical curiosities.

For each problem provide:

1. The problem statement.
2. Field (analysis / topology / probability / geometry / etc).
3. Why it is interesting or classic.
4. The key idea or trick used in the solution (brief).
5. Estimated difficulty (1–5).

Avoid extremely technical problems or problems requiring long calculations.
Prefer problems with one key clever insight.

You may refer to the "materials" folder for materials. I put textbooks, exercise books, classical brain teaser books under the folder, separated by the classification. You may also refer to "The_BOOK.pdf".

# Logistics
- You should not give repeated questions
- All the previous question given is in the README.md table, or in the Problem_list.md under the questions folder.
- You should update the Problem_list.md after you give a question
- Since the original terminal is bad for showing inline markdown, write the stuff like why it's beautiful, difficulty, tags, etc to the question markdown file. Do the same thing for answer. Remember to give a link for fast access (for example: Writtent to answer.md (link to the file))
- If you referred any of the materials, give the reference (citation) of where the question comes from. You don't have to refer to the materials if you did not use the materials
- It is important to build intuition. So always give high level intuition to the approach/answer to the question before diving into the formal regorous answer
