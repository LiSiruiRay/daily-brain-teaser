# The Open Mapping Theorem: A Surprising Consequence

Let $f: \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ be a non-constant entire function. Prove that the image $f(\mathbb{C})$ is **dense** in $\mathbb{C}$.

Now here is the surprise: can you prove the stronger statement that $f(\mathbb{C})$ actually **omits at most one point** of $\mathbb{C}$, using only the Open Mapping Theorem (no Picard)?

For the main problem: suppose $f$ is a non-constant entire function whose image $f(\mathbb{C})$ omits an open disk $D(w_0, r)$. Derive a contradiction using only the Open Mapping Theorem.

*Bonus thought:* What does this tell you about $e^z$?
