# The Holomorphic Function That Misses Two Points

Let $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ be an entire function. Suppose that $f$ omits two distinct values, i.e., there exist $a, b \in \mathbb{C}$ with $a \neq b$ such that $f(z) \neq a$ and $f(z) \neq b$ for all $z \in \mathbb{C}$.

Prove that $f$ must be constant.

*(This is the **Little Picard Theorem**. Your task: give the slickest proof using only tools from a first complex analysis course — specifically, think about what $f$ avoids and how to construct a bounded entire function.)*
