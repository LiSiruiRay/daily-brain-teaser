# The Flippant Juror: Three Jurors and a Coin

A three-person jury must decide the guilt or innocence of a defendant. Two jurors are competent: each independently makes the correct decision with probability $p > \frac{1}{2}$. The third juror is completely useless — she decides by flipping a fair coin (probability $\frac{1}{2}$ of being correct).

The jury uses **majority rule**: the defendant is convicted if and only if at least 2 of the 3 jurors vote "guilty."

Compare the probability that the jury reaches the **correct verdict** under majority rule to the probability that a single **competent** juror reaches the correct decision. Which is larger?

The answer may surprise you.
