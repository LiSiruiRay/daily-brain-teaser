# Random Quadratic Equations: Real Roots Almost Surely?

**(a)** Choose the point $(b,c)$ uniformly at random from the square $[-B,B]^2$. What is the probability $p(B)$ that

$$x^2 + 2bx + c = 0$$

has real roots? What happens to $p(B)$ as $B \to \infty$, so that "$b$ and $c$ can be anything"?

**(b)** Now choose $(a,b,c)$ uniformly at random from the cube $[-B,B]^3$. What is the probability that

$$ax^2 + 2bx + c = 0$$

has real roots? Does the answer depend on $B$?

**(c)** Dividing by $a$ turns the equation in (b) into one of the form in (a). Explain why the two answers are nevertheless completely different.
