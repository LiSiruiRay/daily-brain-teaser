# The Unfair Subway

Marvin finishes work at a **uniformly random** time between 3 pm and 5 pm and walks to the subway station. From there he can go:

- **uptown**, to his mother's, or
- **downtown**, to his girlfriend's.

He has no preference, so he boards **whichever train arrives first**. Trains in each direction run with perfect regularity, **every 10 minutes**.

His mother complains that over many months he has visited her on only about **1 day in 10**.

1. Explain how this can happen even though both directions have identical service.
2. Suppose trains in each direction run every $T$ minutes, and each uptown train arrives $d$ minutes after a downtown train, where $0 < d < T$. Find:
   - $P(\text{Marvin goes uptown})$, and
   - Marvin's expected waiting time.
3. Which offset $d$ minimizes his expected wait? What is his expected wait in Marvin's actual situation?

*(Bonus thought: "equal frequency" sounds like "equal chance." What exactly is the hidden assumption that fails?)*
