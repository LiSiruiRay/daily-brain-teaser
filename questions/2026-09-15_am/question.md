# The Fundamental Group of the Projective Plane Kills Itself When Doubled

Consider the real projective plane $\mathbb{RP}^2$ and the 2-sphere $S^2$.

We know $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

Now consider the **connected sum** $\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$ (two copies of $\mathbb{RP}^2$ glued along a disk).

**(a)** Use the Seifert–Van Kampen theorem to compute $\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2)$.

**(b)** Identify this space: what well-known surface is $\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$ homeomorphic to? What is its fundamental group?

**(c)** Is this group abelian? Does the result surprise you?
