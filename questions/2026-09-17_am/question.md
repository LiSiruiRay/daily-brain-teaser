# The Conformal Map That Doubles Its Angle

Let $f : \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\} \to \mathbb{C}$ be defined by
$$f(z) = z + \frac{1}{z}.$$

**(a)** Show that $f$ maps the upper unit semicircle $\{e^{i\theta} : 0 < \theta < \pi\}$ to the interval $(-2, 2)$ on the real axis.

**(b)** Show that $f$ maps the upper half of any circle $|z| = r$ with $r \neq 1$ to an **ellipse**, and identify its semi-axes.

**(c)** Conclude: what does $f$ do to the family of concentric circles $|z| = r > 0$ and the family of rays $\arg(z) = \theta$? Where do you see this map "in the wild"?
