# Sums of Three Cubes: A Putnam Divisibility Surprise

Let $n$ be a positive integer. Show that there exist integers $a, b, c$ (not necessarily distinct, possibly negative) such that

$$n = a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc + n^3.$$

Wait — that's trivially $n = n^3 + 0 + 0 - 0$... Let's do the real thing.

---

**The actual problem (Putnam 2002, B-1):**

Shanille O'Keal shoots free throws. She hits the first and misses the second, and thereafter the probability that she hits her $n$-th attempt equals the proportion of hits among her first $n-1$ attempts. What is the probability she hits exactly 50 of her first 100 attempts?
