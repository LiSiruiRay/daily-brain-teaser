# The Lazy Prisoner: A Probability Paradox

Three prisoners, A, B, and C, each believe their chance of being pardoned is $\frac{1}{3}$. The warden knows that **exactly one** will be pardoned. Prisoner A asks the warden: *"Please tell me the name of one of B or C who will NOT be pardoned."* The warden agrees — if both B and C are to be executed, he flips a fair coin to decide whom to name.

The warden says: **"B will not be pardoned."**

**Question:** What is the probability that A is pardoned, given this information? What about C?

Many people instinctively say A's probability jumps from $\frac{1}{3}$ to $\frac{1}{2}$ — is this correct?
