# The Three-Door Switcheroo: A Generalized Monty Hall

You are on a game show with $n \geq 3$ doors. Behind one door is a car; the rest hide goats. You pick a door. The host (who knows where the car is) then opens $k$ of the remaining doors, all revealing goats, where $1 \leq k \leq n-2$. The host then offers you the chance to switch to any one of the remaining $n - 1 - k$ unopened doors (not your original).

**Questions:**

1. What is the probability of winning if you **stay**?
2. What is the probability of winning if you **switch** (choosing uniformly at random among the $n-1-k$ remaining doors)?
3. For fixed $n$, as $k$ increases (the host opens more doors), what happens to the advantage of switching?
