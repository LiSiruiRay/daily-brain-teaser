# The Zeros That Refuse to Accumulate

Let $f$ be analytic on the open unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{|z| < 1\}$, and suppose $f$ is not identically zero. 

Define the sequence $z_n = 1 - \frac{1}{n}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Can $f(z_n) = 0$ for all $n$?**

Now consider a different sequence: let $w_n$ be any sequence of points in $\mathbb{D}$ with $|w_n| \to 1$ (approaching the boundary, but not necessarily along a single radial direction).

**Can $f(w_n) = 0$ for all $n$, with $f$ not identically zero?**

Decide each case, and explain precisely why one is impossible and the other is possible — and construct an explicit example for the possible case.
