# The Comb Space Is Contractible — Or Is It?

Consider the **closed cone** over the Hawaiian Earring (sometimes called the "cone" construction). Actually, let's focus on something more elementary and surprising:

Let $X = \{0\} \times [0,1] \cup \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[\tfrac{1}{n+1}, \tfrac{1}{n}\right] \times \{0\}$... 

No — here is the clean problem:

---

Define the **closed topologist's comb** as:

$$C = \left(\{0\} \times [0,1]\right) \cup \left([0,1] \times \{0\}\right) \cup \bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} \left\{\tfrac{1}{n}\right\} \times [0,1]$$

as a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

**(a)** Is $C$ path-connected?

**(b)** Is $C$ contractible (i.e., is the identity map $\mathrm{id}_C$ null-homotopic)?

**(c)** Yet, is the point $p = (0, 1)$ a "strong deformation retract" of any neighborhood of it in $C$?

The surprise: answer **(a)** and **(b)** first — you will find both are **yes** — then explain why **(c)** is **no**, and what this reveals about the difference between contractibility and **local contractibility**.
