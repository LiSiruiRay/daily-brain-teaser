# Successive Wins: Father or Champion in the Middle?

Elmer is promised a prize if he wins **at least two tennis sets in a row** in a three-set series. His opponents alternate between his father and the club champion. He may choose either order:

- **Father – Champion – Father** (F–C–F), or
- **Champion – Father – Champion** (C–F–C).

The champion is the stronger player. Let $f$ be Elmer's probability of beating his father in a set and $c$ his probability of beating the champion, with $0 < c < f < 1$. Assume the sets are independent.

**(a)** Which order should Elmer choose to maximize his chance of the prize?

**(b)** Suppose the rule changes to winning **at least two of the three sets**, consecutive or not. Does the better order change?

*Try to guess before computing. Most people's first instinct for (a) is wrong.*
