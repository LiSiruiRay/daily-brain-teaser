# Answer: The Wedge That Knows Its Loops

## Key Idea / Intuition

The fundamental group of a wedge is the free product of the fundamental groups (by van Kampen's theorem). A connected $n$-sheeted covering space of $X$ corresponds to a subgroup of index $n$ in $\pi_1(X)$. A **2-sheeted** covering exists if and only if $\pi_1(X)$ has a subgroup of index 2 — equivalently, a surjective homomorphism $\pi_1(X) \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. The free product structure makes this easy to detect, and the answer reveals a beautiful interplay between torsion and the abelianization.

---

## Formal Proof / Solution

**Step 1: Compute $\pi_1(X)$.**

By the Seifert–van Kampen theorem, the fundamental group of a wedge $A \vee B$ (with the basepoint at the wedge point) is the **free product**:
$$\pi_1(A \vee B) \cong \pi_1(A) * \pi_1(B).$$

We know:
- $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}P^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $a$ with $a^2 = 1$.
- $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by $b$.

Therefore:
$$\pi_1(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} = \langle a, b \mid a^2 = 1 \rangle.$$

**Step 2: Does a 2-sheeted covering exist?**

A connected 2-sheeted covering of $X$ corresponds exactly to a **normal subgroup of index 2** in $\pi_1(X)$, or equivalently, a surjective homomorphism $\phi: \pi_1(X) \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

Since $\pi_1(X) = \langle a, b \mid a^2 = 1 \rangle$, a homomorphism $\phi$ to $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} = \{0,1\}$ is determined by the images of $a$ and $b$, subject only to $\phi(a)^2 = 0$. In $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ every element satisfies $x^2 = 0$, so **any** assignment of $\phi(a), \phi(b) \in \{0,1\}$ works.

For surjectivity, at least one generator must map to $1$. One natural choice:
$$\phi(a) = 1, \quad \phi(b) = 0.$$

So yes, a connected 2-sheeted covering **does exist**.

**Step 3: Describe the covering space.**

The covering corresponding to $\ker \phi$ (with $\phi(a)=1, \phi(b)=0$) can be described geometrically. The covering of $\mathbb{R}P^2$ corresponding to the unique index-2 subgroup $\{e\} \subset \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is $S^2$ (the universal cover of $\mathbb{R}P^2$). The covering of $S^1$ corresponding to the whole group $\mathbb{Z}$ (index-2 subgroup $2\mathbb{Z}$ ignored since $\phi(b)=0$) is two disjoint copies of $S^1$.

More precisely, the 2-sheeted covering $\tilde{X}$ is:
$$\tilde{X} \cong S^2 \vee S^1 \vee S^1.$$

**Why?** The deck transformation swaps the two sheets. Over the $\mathbb{R}P^2$ factor (which $\phi$ "sees"), the covering lifts to $S^2$. Over the $S^1$ factor (which $\phi$ "doesn't see," since $\phi(b)=0$), the preimage consists of **two copies** of $S^1$, each living in one sheet, both attached at the lifted basepoint. The resulting space is $S^2$ with two circles attached at a common point, i.e., $S^2 \vee S^1 \vee S^1$.

**Sanity check via $\pi_1$:** 
$$\pi_1(S^2 \vee S^1 \vee S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z},$$
which is indeed the index-2 subgroup $\ker\phi = \langle b, aba^{-1} \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}*\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}$ (the subgroup generated by $b$ and the conjugate $aba^{-1} = aba$, since $a^{-1}=a$).

**Summary:**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| $\pi_1(X)$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} = \langle a,b \mid a^2=1\rangle$ |
| 2-sheeted covering exists? | **Yes** |
| Covering space | $S^2 \vee S^1 \vee S^1$ |
