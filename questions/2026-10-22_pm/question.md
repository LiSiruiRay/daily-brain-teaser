# Positive Real Part of the Derivative Forces Injectivity

Let $D \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a **convex** open set, and let $f : D \to \mathbb{C}$ be holomorphic with
$$
\operatorname{Re} f'(z) > 0 \quad \text{for all } z \in D.
$$

**(a)** Prove that $f$ is injective on $D$.

**(b)** Deduce that
$$
f(z) = z + \frac{z^2}{2}
$$
is injective on the open unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{|z| < 1\}$. Then show that $\mathbb{D}$ is the largest disk centered at $0$ with this property: for every $r > 1$, $f$ fails to be injective on $\{|z| < r\}$.

*(Hint: think of the real-variable fact that a function with positive derivative on an interval is strictly increasing. Which quantity should play the role of "increasing" here?)*
